    Course Description
    🔗
    A symbolic, numeric, and graphic approach to intermediate algebra with an emphasis on applications. Topics include operations, equations, and inequalities of linear, exponential, logarithmic, quadratic, rational, and radical functions. Please see the mathematics program for placement test details.
    🔗
    Prerequisite
    🔗
    MTH 097 or assignment through GVSU Math Placement. If you think you have been placed in the wrong course, please see me after class today to discuss your placement. You can take a practice proficiency exam: gvsu.edu/s/Ik .To learn about how to take the exam watch: gvsu.edu/s/jq. To take the actual proficiency exam: gvsu.edu/s/jk. It is free and I will not know that you took it.
    🔗
    Textbook (Optional for reference)
    🔗
    Intermediate Algebra, Functions and Authentic Applications by Lehmann published by Pearson, 5th edition.
    🔗
    🔗

    Topics.

    • Reframing Mathematical Mindset (1-2 week)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Exponential Functions (3 - 5 weeks)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Inverse Functions and Logarithmic Functions (2 - 4 weeks)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Quadratic Functions (3 - 5 weeks)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Rational and Radical Functions (1 - 2 weeks)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Course Objectives.

    After successful completion of the course, students will be able to…
    1. Analyze realistic or real-world situations in a mathematical way, using exponential and quadratic models.
      🔗
    2. Select appropriate variables to represent the functional inputs and outputs for a mathematical model of a situation using exponential and quadratic models.
      🔗
    3. Create an appropriate mathematical model from a description or data, using, if necessary, appropriate technological tools for exponential and quadratic models.
      🔗
    4. Solve problems involving exponential, logarithmic, and quadratic equations.
      🔗
    5. Translate between different representations of functions (algebraic, graphical, and tabular)
      🔗
    6. Explain the mathematical reasoning involved in solving a problem both orally and in writing.
      🔗

    Subsection Structure and Flow of the Course

    Your MTH 110 course is being offered in a face-to-face (F2F) mode. The content of MTH 110 is divided into 15 modules (one module per week). Each of which is divided into two parts (one for each class meeting). Your engagement and work on each module follow a standard pattern:
    • Before each class meeting, you need to go to the corresponding day and complete the Prep Work for that day. Prep work involves watching screencasts that leads you through independent learning of the basic ideas of new material, then complete their exit preview quiz.
      🔗
      🔗
    • During our class meeting, you will participate in active work on applications and analysis of the basic information you learned in the Preview assignment. Your attendance is important for the group and for your learning.
      🔗
      🔗
    • There is a weekly online homework on WebWork (see below). This is typically due at 11PM on Saturdays. You need to pace yourself during the week and finish a few problems each day. Office hours is a great place to work on that with the rest of the group.
      🔗

You will get instant feedback on your answers, and you will have the opportunity to attempt any homework question an unlimited number of times before its deadline.     🔗
    In-Class Activities 30%
    🔗
    Every meeting, we will have an in-class group activity. Such activities will be completed in groups, where each student will receive the same grade. The goal of those activities is to get a hands-on experience with topics being discussed. Missing a class means that you forfeit the points of those activities. I will drop the lowest two activities in case you had to miss class that day. No excuses
    🔗
    Preview Activities 5%
    🔗
    Before class, there are some screencasts that I ask you to watch and then take simple 2-3 question activity on Bb.
    🔗
    Learning Targets 50%
    🔗
    This class will use a mastery-based testing policy. The mastery-based testing works like this: The course material is broken into 19 main Learning Targets (LTs), See the Learning Targets section below. This is a rather rigid scoring system, where partial credit is not awarded. The benefit is that students will have multiple attempts to demonstrate their mastery of the material at no cost for failing to do so on their first attempt.
    🔗
    Every other Thursday you will have a checkpoint to attempt 1-2 LTs, with the opportunity to re-attempt the LTs from the previous checkpoint. If you still did not reach mastery, you can re-attempt the LTs you need during my office hours. Your last chance to attempt all LTs will be on your final exam which scheduled for Thursday, December 17, 8:00 am - 9:50 am.
    🔗
    After all four exams are completed, each student will have a number of mastered topics out of the 19 main topics. The percentage of mastered topics is the exam score for that student and will account for 50% of the course grade as per the grade distribution above. There is no penalty for mastering a LT on the second, third, or fourth exam after failing to do so on a previous exam. If students demonstrate that they’ve mastered a topic at any point during the semester, they receive full credit for doing so.
    🔗
    The grading of the LTs will be tough (no partial credit will be given) since it is an indication if the student has mastered the target or not. The benefit of this system is that students are not punished if they fail to master a topic but over the course of the semester demonstrate that they have mastered the topic.

    Subsection Learning Targets

    🔗
    1. [Functions] I can sketch qualitative graphs with appropriate variable definitions.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. [Functions] I can describe a situation given a qualitative graph with defined inputs and outputs.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. [Linear] Given a data set, make a linear regression model on Desmos, identify and explain the meaning of slope in the context, identify and explain the y-intercept in the context, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. [Linear] Given a linear graph, identify the slope in the context, the vertical intercept, horizontal intercept, evaluate \(f(a)\text{,}\) find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and make the equation of the line.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. [Linear] Given a linear word situation, make an equation, identify and explain the meaning of slope in the context, identify and explain the y-intercept in the context, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. [Linear] Given a system of linear equations, or a word situation, or data sets create a system of equations as applicable and solve the system.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. [Linear] Given a linear function, find the inverse symbolically, explain how the domain and range is affected, and evaluate function notation questions.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. [Linear] I can recognize and identify key characteristics of a linear pattern represented in a table, graph, equation or application. I can translate between different representations.
      🔗
      🔗
    9. [Exponential] Given a data set, make an exponential regression model on Desmos, identify and explain the meaning of growth/decay rate in the context, identify and explain the y-intercept in the context, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning.
      🔗
      🔗
    10. [Exponential] Given an exponential graph, identify the growth/decay rate, the vertical intercept, evaluate \(f(a)\text{,}\) and find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and make an equation for the curve.
      🔗
      🔗
    11. [Exponential] Given an exponential word situation, such as half life, compound interest, depreciation, or simple growth/decay, make an equation, identify and explain the meaning of growth/decay rate in the context, identify and explain the y-intercept in the context, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
      🔗
    12. [Exponential] I can recognize and identify key characteristics of an exponential pattern represented in a table, graph, equation or application. I can translate between different representations.
      🔗
      🔗
    13. [Exponential] Solve equations and word situations involving exponential, logarithmic, and power functions symbolically and with technology.
      🔗
      🔗
    14. [Quadratic] Given a data set, make a quadratic regression model on Desmos, identify and explain the meaning of x-intercepts, y-intercepts, and vertex in the context,find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning.
      🔗
      🔗
    15. [Quadratic] Given a quadratic graph, find the equation in 3 different forms, identify the horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex, evaluate \(f(a)\text{,}\) and find \(x\) given \(f(a)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    16. [Quadratic] Given a quadratic word situation, graph the model on Desmos, identify and explain the meaning of x-intercepts, y-intercepts, and vertex in the context, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning,and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
      🔗
    17. [Quadratic] I can recognize and identify key characteristics of a quadratic pattern represented in a table, graph, equation or application. I can translate between different representations.
      🔗
      🔗
    18. [Radical] Given a radical application, find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning,and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
      🔗
    19. [Rational] Given a rational application, find an equation, and find \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning,and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
      🔗

    Subsection University and Class Policies

    This course is subject to the GVSU policies listed at http://www.gvsu.edu/coursepolicies/
    🔗
    Attendance
    🔗
    At Grand Valley, regular attendance in class and other course activities is considered an essential part of the students’ educational experience and a requirement for an adequate evaluation of student academic progress.
    🔗
    Coursework missed because of excused absences should be made up to the satisfaction of the instructor. Although makeup work will not remove the full adverse effect of the absence in all cases, faculty members will make reasonable accommodations for students when an absence is excused. The accommodations available will vary with course and program. The degree of the effect upon grades will vary with the nature and amount of work missed and must be measured according to the instructor’s best judgment. In case of excessive absences, the instructor may refuse to grant credit for the course. Under some circumstances, an incomplete grade followed by a resolution according to university policy, or withdrawal from the course, is appropriate.
    🔗
    Absences due to the following reasons should be treated as excused: 1) absences of students who miss class due to active participation in an intercollegiate event; 2) absences due to the observance of religious holidays, see PC 9.6, Religious Inclusion Policy ; 3) absences due to military duty; 4) absences due to jury duty, or appearance in court or other government hearings; 5) student medical conditions that preclude class attendance; 6) birth or adoption of child; 7) absences due to attendance at an academic and/or professional conference; and 8) bereavement for an individual of significant relationship. Instructors have discretion to determine whether absences for other reasons should be treated as excused (e.g. study abroad program).
    🔗
    Regardless of the reason, I am required to report a full week of absence on Navigate.
    🔗
    Academic Honesty
    🔗
    Some of the work in this course will be done collaboratively. It may be particularly helpful to meet once a week with other students to summarize and review key ideas in the course; indeed, this is essential to the semester project. Even on homework, some collaboration is permitted. However, please understand that there is a difference between collaboration and plagiarism. Collaboration requires you to contribute to solutions and think when you write. Copying any portion of someone else’s work, no matter how small, is plagiarism! Evidence of plagiarism in an assignment will result in a minimum penalty of zero on the paper and possible action under the guidelines of the GVSU catalog. You are expected to show integrity in all your work, and to encourage your peers to do likewise. I reserve the right to discuss the nature of your work with you prior to deciding a grade on an assignment.
    🔗
    AI Policy
    🔗
    SLT 11.16 outlines permitted and non-permitted uses of AI at GVSU for students, faculty and staff. In MTH 110, students are not allowed to use advanced automated tools (artificial intelligence or machine learning tools such as ChatGPT or Dall-E 2) on assignments in this course. Each student is expected to complete each assignment without substantive assistance from others, including automated tools. You are not alone; you have the support of your dedicated faculty whose ultimate goal is make sure you are successful.
